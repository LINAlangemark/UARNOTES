## How a Rotor Produces Thrust

Each of the four rotors is driven by an electric motor to spin at angular velocity $\omega_i$ (in rad/s). As the rotor blades spin through the air they accelerate air downward — this reaction force pushes the drone upward. The magnitude of this **thrust force** is:

$$
f_i = k\, \omega_i^2
$$

where $k$ is the **thrust (lift) coefficient**, a constant that depends on the blade geometry, air density, and blade pitch angle. The quadratic dependence on $\omega_i$ comes from aerodynamic lift theory: doubling the rotor speed produces four times the thrust.

All four thrust forces act along the body $z_B$-axis (perpendicular to the rotor plane):

$$
T_B =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
T
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The total thrust $T$ is simply the sum of individual rotor thrusts:

$$
T \quad = \quad \sum_{i=1}^{4} f_i  \quad = \quad k \sum_{i=1}^{4} \omega_i^2
$$

Because all thrust acts along $z_B$, the quadrotor steers by **tilting its body** to redirect this thrust vector — it has no ability to generate side thrust directly.

---

## How a Rotor Produces Drag Torque

A spinning rotor does not just produce thrust — it also experiences **aerodynamic drag** opposing its rotation. By Newton's third law, if the air resists the rotor spinning in one direction, the rotor exerts an equal and opposite torque on the drone body. 
This is the **reaction torque** or **drag torque**:

$$
\tau_{M_i} = b\, \omega_i^2 + I_M\, \dot{\omega}_i
$$

where:
- $b$ is the **drag coefficient** (analogous to $k$ but for torque)
- $I_M$ is the **rotor moment of inertia** (resistance of the spinning mass to angular acceleration)
- $\dot{\omega}_i$ is the angular acceleration of rotor $i$

In practice, the $I_M \dot{\omega}_i$ term is small compared to the aerodynamic drag term and is often **neglected** in the model, simplifying to $\tau_{M_i} \approx b\, \omega_i^2$.

---

## Rotor Spin Directions and Yaw

If all four rotors spun in the same direction, their **drag torques** would all act the **same way** on the body — causing the drone to **spin uncontrollably** about $z_B$. To prevent this, the quadrotor uses alternating spin directions:

- Rotors 1 and 3 spin in one direction (e.g. counterclockwise, when viewed from above)
- Rotors 2 and 4 spin in the opposite direction (clockwise)

During steady hover, these torques cancel exactly and the drone does not yaw. To intentionally yaw, the controller increases the speeds of one diagonal pair and decreases the other, creating a net torque about $z_B$.

---

## Body Torques from Rotor Speed Differences

Let $l$ be the distance from the centre of mass to each rotor. The torques that rotate the drone about its body axes are produced by *differences* in rotor speeds, not by their sum.

### Roll Torque $\tau_\phi$

Roll is a rotation about $x_B$ — the drone tips left or right. Rotors 2 (left) and 4 (right) control this:

$$
\tau_\phi = l\, k\left(-\omega_2^2 + \omega_4^2\right)
$$

Speeding up rotor 4 (right side) while slowing rotor 2 (left side) produces a net force imbalance that rolls the drone to the left.

### Pitch Torque $\tau_\theta$

Pitch is a rotation about $y_B$ — the drone tips forward or backward. Rotors 1 (front) and 3 (rear) control this:

$$
\tau_\theta = l\, k\left(-\omega_1^2 + \omega_3^2\right)
$$

Speeding up rotor 3 (rear) while slowing rotor 1 (front) pitches the nose down.

### Yaw Torque $\tau_\psi$

Yaw is a rotation about $z_B$ — the drone rotates its heading. It is generated by the sum of all drag torques, which no longer cancel when one diagonal pair is faster than the other:

$$
\tau_\psi = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \tau_{M_i} = b\left(\omega_1^2 - \omega_2^2 + \omega_3^2 - \omega_4^2\right)
$$

(using the sign convention where rotors 1, 3 and rotors 2, 4 spin in opposite directions).

### Combined Torque Vector

All three body torques are collected into:

$$
\tau_B =
\begin{bmatrix}
\tau_\phi \\[4pt]
\tau_\theta \\[4pt]
\tau_\psi
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
l\, k\left(-\omega_2^2 + \omega_4^2\right) \\[4pt]
l\, k\left(-\omega_1^2 + \omega_3^2\right) \\[4pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4} \tau_{M_i}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## The Four Control Actions

From $T$ and $\tau_B$ we can see that the four rotor speeds $\omega_1, \omega_2, \omega_3, \omega_4$ produce exactly **four independent control inputs**:

| Control output | Physical effect | Rotor combination |
|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| $T$ (total thrust) | Vertical acceleration | All four speeds up/down together |
| $\tau_\phi$ (roll torque) | Left/right tilt | $\omega_4$ vs. $\omega_2$ |
| $\tau_\theta$ (pitch torque) | Forward/backward tilt | $\omega_3$ vs. $\omega_1$ |
| $\tau_\psi$ (yaw torque) | Heading rotation | $\omega_1+\omega_3$ vs. $\omega_2+\omega_4$ |

These four outputs must simultaneously regulate six degrees of freedom ($x$, $y$, $z$, $\phi$, $\theta$, $\psi$), which is why the quadrotor is underactuated. The horizontal positions $x$ and $y$ can only be controlled *indirectly* by first rolling or pitching the drone to redirect the thrust.

---

## Worked Example: Roll Torque Calculation

Given rotor speeds:

$$
\omega_1 = \omega_3 = 500\ \text{rad/s}, \quad \omega_2 = 400\ \text{rad/s}, \quad \omega_4 = 600\ \text{rad/s}
$$

and parameters:

$$
l = 0.2\ \text{m}, \quad k = 1 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{N·s}^2/\text{rad}^2
$$

Roll torque:

$$
\tau_\phi = l\, k\left(-\omega_2^2 + \omega_4^2\right) = 0.2 \times 10^{-5} \times \left(-400^2 + 600^2\right)
$$

$$
= 0.2 \times 10^{-5} \times \left(-160\,000 + 360\,000\right) = 0.2 \times 10^{-5} \times 200\,000
$$

$$
= 0.40\ \text{N·m}
$$

This positive roll torque would cause the drone to rotate about $x_B$, tilting it to one side.

## Quick Reference

| Symbol | Meaning | Units |
|--------|---------|-------|
| $\omega_i$ | Angular velocity of rotor $i$ | rad/s |
| $k$ | Thrust (lift) coefficient | N·s²/rad² |
| $b$ | Drag coefficient | N·m·s²/rad² |
| $f_i = k\omega_i^2$ | Thrust of rotor $i$ | N |
| $T = \sum f_i$ | Total thrust | N |
| $\tau_{M_i} = b\omega_i^2$ | Drag torque of rotor $i$ | N·m |
| $l$ | Rotor distance from centre of mass | m |
| $\tau_\phi,\ \tau_\theta,\ \tau_\psi$ | Roll, pitch, yaw torques | N·m |
| $I_M$ | Rotor moment of inertia | kg·m² |
