Chapter 2 the report defines the quadrotor’s coordinate frames, state variables, and basic kinematic and force equations.

Two coordinate frames are used:

- **Inertial (world) frame** with axes ($x, y, z$)
- **Body (quadrotor) frame** with axes ($x_B, y_B, z_B$) centered at the vehicle’s center of mass

The reason two frames are required is that **the drone moves through space while also rotating**. Some quantities are easiest to describe in the **fixed world frame** (position and gravity), while others are naturally expressed in the **body frame** (forces, thrust, and angular motion).

Position in inertial space is
$$
\xi =
\begin{bmatrix}
x \\
y \\
z
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Attitude is expressed with Euler angles
$$
\eta =
\begin{bmatrix}
\phi \\
\theta \\
\psi
\end{bmatrix}
$$

_In drone terminology, "**Attitude**" (often abbreviated as ATTI mode) refers to the orientation of the aircraft in relation to the horizon (its pitch, roll, and yaw) rather than its position relative to the ground_

Linear and angular velocities are expressed either in the inertial frame or the body frame.

**Body-frame linear velocity**:

$$
V_B =
\begin{bmatrix}
v_{x,B} \\
v_{y,B} \\
v_{z,B}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Body angular velocity**:

$$
\nu =
\begin{bmatrix}
p \\
q \\
r
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The **rotation matrix** ($R(\phi,\theta,\psi)$) maps vectors from the body frame to the inertial frame.

This matrix is necessary because when the drone tilts, the direction of thrust changes relative to the world. The rotation matrix mathematically describes **how the drone’s orientation rotates body vectors into world coordinates**.

The relation between Euler angle rates and body angular rates is given by a transformation matrix ($W_\eta$).

Rotor thrust and torque are defined as
$$
f_i = k\omega_i^2
$$
$$
\tau_{M_i} = b\omega_i^2
$$

Total thrust and torques acting on the body are

$$
T = \sum_{i=1}^4 f_i
$$

and

$$
\tau_\phi, \tau_\theta, \tau_\psi
$$

which are generated by differences in rotor speeds.

These forces and torques form the **control inputs of the quadrotor system**, allowing the vehicle to regulate both orientation and translational motion.

---

# Coordinate Frames and State Variables

## Inertial Frame

A fixed Earth reference frame with axes ($x,y,z$).

This frame does **not move with the drone**. It is used to measure:

- position
- velocity relative to the ground
- gravitational force

The quadrotor position is

$$
\xi =
\begin{bmatrix}
x \\
y \\
z
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Gravity acts along the inertial $z$-axis:

$$
G =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
-g
\end{bmatrix}
$$

This vector represents gravitational acceleration acting downward.

Because gravity always acts vertically relative to Earth, it is most naturally expressed in the **inertial frame**.

---

## Body Frame

A frame attached to the quadrotor with origin at its center of mass.

Because this frame **moves and rotates with the vehicle**, it is ideal for describing:

- rotor thrust
- aerodynamic forces
- angular motion

Axes:

- $x_B$
- $y_B$
- $z_B$

The ($z_B$) axis points in the thrust direction.

The orientation of this body frame relative to the inertial frame is described using Euler angles:

$$
\eta =
\begin{bmatrix}
\phi \\
\theta \\
\psi
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where

- ( $\phi$ ) = roll (rotation about ($x_B$))
- ( $\theta$ ) = pitch (rotation about ($y_B$))
- ( $\psi$ ) = yaw (rotation about ($z_B$))

These angles describe how the quadrotor body is rotated relative to the world.

For example:

- roll tilts the drone sideways
- pitch tilts it forward/backward
- yaw rotates the heading direction

---

## State Vector

The full state vector combines position and attitude:

$$
q =
\begin{bmatrix}
\xi \\
\eta
\end{bmatrix}
$$

or explicitly

$$
q =
\begin{bmatrix}
x \\
y \\
z \\
\phi \\
\theta \\
\psi
\end{bmatrix}
$$

This vector defines the **configuration of the quadrotor in space**.

Since the quadrotor has six independent coordinates, it has **six degrees of freedom**.

---

# Velocities

Motion of the quadrotor includes both **translation** and **rotation**.

## Linear Velocity in Body Frame

$$
V_B =
\begin{bmatrix}
v_{x,B} \\
v_{y,B} \\
v_{z,B}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

These are velocity components measured **along the body axes**.

For example:

- $v_{x,B}$ is forward velocity
- $v_{y,B}$ is sideways velocity
- $v_{z,B}$ is vertical velocity relative to the drone

Expressing velocity in the body frame is useful because aerodynamic forces and thrust are naturally aligned with the body axes.

---

## Angular Velocity (Body Rates)

$$
\nu =
\begin{bmatrix}
p \\
q \\
r
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where

- $(p)$ = roll rate  
- $(q)$ = pitch rate  
- $(r)$ = yaw rate  

These represent **rotational velocities around the body axes**.

They are measured directly by gyroscopes in the drone’s IMU.

---

# Rotation Matrix

The rotation matrix converts body-frame vectors to inertial coordinates.

Using the yaw–pitch–roll convention:

$$
R = R_z(\psi)R_y(\theta)R_x(\phi)
$$

This means rotations are applied in the order:

1. roll
2. pitch
3. yaw

Expanded:

$$
R =
\begin{bmatrix}
C_\psi C_\theta &
C_\psi S_\theta S_\phi - S_\psi C_\phi &
C_\psi S_\theta C_\phi + S_\psi S_\phi \\

S_\psi C_\theta &
S_\psi S_\theta S_\phi + C_\psi C_\phi &
S_\psi S_\theta C_\phi - C_\psi S_\phi \\

-S_\theta &
C_\theta S_\phi &
C_\theta C_\phi
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where

$$
S_x = \sin(x), \quad C_x = \cos(x)
$$

The matrix is orthogonal:

$$
R^{-1} = R^T
$$

This property means the inverse transformation can be computed simply by transposing the matrix.

---

## Velocity Transformation

Velocity expressed in the body frame can be converted to the inertial frame using

$$
V_I = R V_B
$$

This transformation is necessary because:

- the drone's sensors measure velocities in the **body frame**
- navigation and control often require velocities in the **world frame**

The rotation matrix therefore acts as a **coordinate transformation operator**.

---

# Euler Angle Rates and Body Rates

Euler angle rates:

$$
\dot{\eta} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot{\phi} \\
\dot{\theta} \\
\dot{\psi}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Body angular velocity:

$$
\nu =
\begin{bmatrix}
p \\
q \\
r
\end{bmatrix}
$$

These quantities are **not identical**.

The Euler angle rates describe **how the orientation angles change**, while body rates describe **actual rotational velocity about the body axes**.

They are related by

$$
\dot{\eta} = W_\eta^{-1} \nu
$$

and

$$
\nu = W_\eta \dot{\eta}
$$

This transformation arises because Euler rotations are applied sequentially, which couples the angular velocities.

---

## Euler Rate Transformation

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot{\phi} \\
\dot{\theta} \\
\dot{\psi}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & \sin\phi \tan\theta & \cos\phi \tan\theta \\
0 & \cos\phi & -\sin\phi \\
0 & \frac{\sin\phi}{\cos\theta} & \frac{\cos\phi}{\cos\theta}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
p \\
q \\
r
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Inverse relation:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
p \\
q \\
r
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & -\sin\theta \\
0 & \cos\phi & \cos\theta \sin\phi \\
0 & -\sin\phi & \cos\theta \cos\phi
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot{\phi} \\
\dot{\theta} \\
\dot{\psi}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

This transformation becomes singular when

$$
\cos\theta = 0
$$

which corresponds to a pitch angle of $90^\circ$ (a phenomenon known as **gimbal lock**).

---

# Inertia Matrix

Because the quadrotor is symmetric about the $(x)$ and $(y)$ axes:

$$
I_{xx} = I_{yy}
$$

The inertia tensor becomes

$$
I =
\begin{bmatrix}
I_{xx} & 0 & 0 \\
0 & I_{yy} & 0 \\
0 & 0 & I_{zz}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

This simplification occurs because:

- the mass distribution is symmetric
- cross-products of inertia vanish

The inertia matrix describes how strongly the quadrotor resists **angular acceleration** about each axis.

---

# Rotor Forces and Torques

Each rotor spinning with angular velocity $( \omega_i )$ produces thrust

$$
f_i = k\omega_i^2
$$

This quadratic relationship arises from **aerodynamic lift theory**, where lift is proportional to the square of the blade tip velocity.

Each rotor also produces aerodynamic drag torque

$$
\tau_{M_i} = b\omega_i^2 + I_M \dot{\omega}_i
$$

where

- $(k)$ = thrust coefficient  
- $(b)$ = drag coefficient  
- $(I_M)$ = rotor inertia

Often the $(I_M \dot{\omega}_i)$ term is neglected because rotor acceleration is small relative to aerodynamic drag.

---

## Reaction Torque

The drag torque acting on the rotor produces an **equal and opposite torque on the drone body** due to Newton's third law.

This is called **reaction torque**.

If all rotors spun in the same direction, these torques would cause the drone to spin uncontrollably. To prevent this, quadrotors use alternating rotor directions:

- two rotors rotate clockwise
- two rotate counterclockwise

This arrangement cancels the reaction torques during hover.

---

# Total Thrust

Total thrust is the sum of rotor forces:

$$
T = \sum_{i=1}^{4} f_i = k\sum_{i=1}^{4}\omega_i^2
$$

The thrust vector in body coordinates is

$$
T_B =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
T
\end{bmatrix}
$$

This thrust always acts along the **body $z_B$ axis**.

The quadrotor therefore changes its direction of motion by **tilting**, which rotates this thrust vector.

---

# Body Torques

Let ($l$) be the distance from the center of mass to each rotor.

Roll torque:

$$
\tau_\phi = l k(-\omega_2^2 + \omega_4^2)
$$

Pitch torque:

$$
\tau_\theta = l k(-\omega_1^2 + \omega_3^2)
$$

Yaw torque:

$$
\tau_\psi = \sum_{i=1}^4 \tau_{M_i}
$$

Yaw torque results from the **difference in rotor drag torques**.

Combined torque vector:

$$
\tau_B =
\begin{bmatrix}
\tau_\phi \\
\tau_\theta \\
\tau_\psi
\end{bmatrix}
$$

These torques allow the quadrotor to control its orientation.

---

# Relation to Newton–Euler Dynamics

These definitions lead directly to the quadrotor rigid-body equations.

Newton's second law in body coordinates:

$$
m\dot{V}_B + \nu \times (mV_B) = R^T G + T_B
$$

The cross-product term appears because the body frame is rotating.

In inertial coordinates:

$$
m\ddot{\xi} = G + R T_B
$$

The rotation matrix converts thrust from the body frame into the inertial frame before applying Newton’s second law.

These equations form the foundation for **quadrotor dynamic modeling, simulation, and controller design**.

$$
R =

# Newton–Euler Dynamics

### Translational

$$
m\ddot{\xi} = G + R T_B
$$

Body frame:

$$
m\dot{V}_B + \nu \times (m V_B) = R^T G + T_B
$$

### Rotational

$$
J \dot{\nu} + \nu \times (J\nu) = \tau_B
$$

---

# Euler–Lagrange Formulation

Kinetic energy:

$$
T_\text{trans} = \frac{1}{2} m (\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2 + \dot{z}^2)
$$

$$
T_\text{rot} = \frac{1}{2} \nu^T J \nu
$$

Potential energy:

$$
V = m g z
$$

Lagrangian:

$$
L = T - V
$$

Euler–Lagrange equation:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i} \right) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i} = Q_i
$$

---

# Rotational Dynamics with Coriolis Matrix

Angular accelerations:

$$
\ddot{\eta} = J^{-1} (\tau_B - C(\eta, \dot{\eta}) \dot{\eta})
$$

where $C(\eta, \dot{\eta})$ is the Coriolis/gyroscopic matrix.

---

## Coriolis Matrix \(C(\eta, \dot{\eta})\)

$$
C(\eta, \dot{\eta}) =
\begin{bmatrix}
C_{11} & C_{12} & C_{13} \\
C_{21} & C_{22} & C_{23} \\
C_{31} & C_{32} & C_{33}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

with components:

$$
C_{11} = 0
$$

$$
C_{12} = (I_{yy} - I_{zz})(\dot{\theta} C_\phi S_\phi + \dot{\psi} S_{2\phi} C_\theta) + (I_{zz} - I_{yy}) \dot{\psi} C_{2\phi} C_\theta - I_{xx} \dot{\psi} C_\theta
$$

$$
C_{13} = (I_{zz} - I_{yy}) \dot{\psi} C_\phi S_\phi C_{2\theta}
$$

$$
C_{21} = (I_{zz} - I_{yy})(\dot{\theta} C_\phi S_\phi + \dot{\psi} S_\phi C_\theta) + (I_{yy} - I_{zz}) \dot{\psi} C_{2\phi} C_\theta + I_{xx} \dot{\psi} C_\theta
$$

$$
C_{22} = (I_{zz} - I_{yy}) \dot{\phi} C_\phi S_\phi
$$

$$
C_{23} = -I_{xx} \dot{\psi} S_\theta C_\theta + I_{yy} \dot{\psi} S_{2\phi} S_\theta C_\theta + I_{zz} \dot{\psi} C_{2\phi} S_\theta C_\theta
$$

$$
C_{31} = (I_{yy} - I_{zz}) \dot{\psi} C_{2\theta} S_\phi C_\phi - I_{xx} \dot{\theta} C_\theta
$$

$$
C_{32} = (I_{zz} - I_{yy})(\dot{\theta} C_\phi S_\phi S_\theta + \dot{\phi} S_\phi^2 C_\theta) + (I_{yy} - I_{zz}) \dot{\phi} C_\phi^2 C_\theta + I_{xx} \dot{\psi} S_\theta C_\theta - I_{yy} \dot{\psi} S_\phi^2 S_\theta C_\theta - I_{zz} \dot{\psi} C_\phi^2 S_\theta C_\theta
$$

$$
C_{33} = (I_{yy} - I_{zz}) \dot{\phi} C_\phi S_\phi C_\theta^2 - I_{yy} \dot{\theta} S_\phi^2 C_\theta S_\theta - I_{zz} \dot{\theta} C_\phi^2 C_\theta S_\theta + I_{xx} \dot{\theta} C_\theta S_\theta
$$
